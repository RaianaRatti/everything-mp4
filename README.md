# Everything MP4

Everything MP4 is a website for doing things with videos. All processing happens in the browser,
so your files are never uploaded.

## Features

Every tool works the same way: drop in a file, press one button, and download the result. Less
common settings are tucked away under **advanced**. Home has a search box for finding tools
(press Enter to open the first match), and you can drop a file anywhere on Home to see the
tools that can work with it. Every result has an **open in…** menu to send it to another tool
without downloading it. The settings (gear, top right) show the size of the video engine and
speech models kept on this device, with a button to delete each, and the licenses.

### Convert

- **Convert:** change a video's format. MP4, MOV, WebM and MKV are made by the browser itself;
  older and specialist formats (AVI, WMV, FLV, MPG, VOB, MPEG-TS, M2TS, 3GP, OGV, ProRes, DNxHR
  and lossless FFV1) use the video engine. Drop several files to convert them one after another
  with the same settings.
  - **Presets:** one click for plays everywhere, web, iPhone, Android, old DVD player, video
    editing or archive.
  - **Settings:** quality (smaller, balanced or high), and under advanced the video and audio
    codecs, resolution and frame rate. You can also remove the sound.
  - Before you start, it tells you whether it will be **instant** (the video is copied as it is,
    with no quality lost), **fast** (this device's video encoder) or needs the **video engine**,
    and about how big the result will be.
  - Files with the wrong extension (say, a MOV named .mp4) are recognized by what's inside them.
    Files the browser can't play show a few still frames instead.
- **Compress:** make a video smaller. Aim for a size (10, 25, 50 or 100 MB, or your own), set a
  quality, or say how much smaller (say, 50%). For very small targets it also lowers the
  resolution, frame rate or sound quality, and tells you. If the result comes out too big, it
  tries again a little smaller. Compare before and after with a slider.
- **Video to GIF:** turn part of a video into an animated GIF. Choose the width, frames per
  second, speed, how many times it plays, boomerang (forward then backward), the number of colors
  and the dithering. You see a size estimate while you choose and the result before you download
  it. It can also make an animated WebP, which is much smaller.
- **Frames:** save the frame on screen at full resolution as PNG, JPEG or WebP, or copy it.
  Step frame by frame with the buttons or the , and . keys. Or pull out many frames as a ZIP
  (a number evenly spread, one every few seconds, only keyframes, or every frame in a part), or
  make a contact sheet: a grid of thumbnails with their times and the file's details.

- **Post:** get a video ready for YouTube, YouTube Shorts, TikTok, Instagram (Reels,
  Stories, square or 4:5 feed), Facebook, X, LinkedIn, Snapchat, WhatsApp, Discord or email. It
  sets the right shape and fits the video with a blurred background (or black bars, a crop you
  can slide, or a stretch), with a live preview. Too long? Keep the start or split it into parts.
  Too big? It's compressed to fit.

### Edit

- **Merge:** pick a part of each of two or more videos (say 1:00–3:20 of the first and
  0:30–2:00 of the second) and play them at the same time in one MP4.
  - **Layout:** overlay the others on the first (picture-in-picture), put them side by side,
    stack them, or (with three or more) arrange them in a grid. Drag a video's box in the preview
    to move it and drag its corners to resize it (hold Shift to change its proportions), or type
    exact positions. Choose which videos are on top and whether each fits whole, is cropped to
    fill its box, or is stretched.
  - **Different lengths:** the result lasts as long as the longest part. When a shorter one runs
    out, its box turns black.
  - **Audio:** set each video's volume from 0 to 200%. All of them are mixed into the result.
  - **Output:** match any video's size or pick 720p, 1080p, 4K, vertical or square. You can
    also choose the frame rate and quality.

- **Trim:** pick one part or several on the timeline (drag the handles, type the times, or press
  I and O), then keep them or cut them out. **Fast** is instant with no quality loss but cuts at
  the nearest keyframe; **exact** cuts exactly where you chose. Save one file or one per part.
- **Split:** cut a video into parts every few seconds, into equal parts, by file size, at
  markers you add, or at scene changes it finds for you (turn each one on or off). Parts are
  named name_part01, name_part02… and download as a ZIP.
- **Crop:** cut away the edges. Type the box or pick a shape (16:9, 9:16, 1:1, 4:5, 4:3, 21:9),
  see the result live, or remove black bars automatically.
- **Resize:** 4K, 1080p, 720p, 480p or 360p, a width, a height, a percent, or your own size. A
  new shape is filled with black or colored bars, blurred video, a crop, or a stretch.
- **Rotate:** turn 90°, 180° or 270°, mirror, flip, or straighten a slight tilt (it zooms in just
  enough to hide the corners). Plain turns of MP4 and MOV are instant and lossless; you can also
  bake the turn in for players that ignore it.
- **Speed:** 0.25× to 8×, for the whole video or one part. The voice stays natural, changes
  pitch, or is muted. Slow motion can blend frames to look smoother. Or make a timelapse by
  keeping every Nth frame or aiming for a length.
- **Reverse & loop:** play a video backwards (sound too), make a boomerang, or repeat it a number
  of times or up to a length (instant, no quality loss).
- **Join:** put videos one after another. Reorder them, trim each one and set its volume. Pick
  the output size and how clips that don't fit are framed. Same-format clips join instantly with
  no quality loss; otherwise it says why it re-encodes. Add a crossfade, fade through black,
  wipe or slide between clips; the sound crossfades too.

### Audio

- **Extract audio:** save a video's sound as MP3, M4A, WAV, FLAC or OGG (Opus), or as AIFF,
  ALAC or WMA with the video engine, or as an iPhone ringtone (M4R, up to 40 seconds). It also
  converts audio files. When the sound can be kept as it is, it's copied instantly with no
  quality lost. Keep just a part, pick the quality, and under advanced the sample rate, mono or
  stereo, and which audio track. Add a title, artist, album and cover art (the video's current
  frame or an image) where the format allows.

- **Edit audio:** mute a video, change its volume (0–300%) with fades, replace its sound with
  another file (start it later, loop it, fade it), or add background music that automatically
  gets quieter while someone talks. Fix audio that's out of sync by moving it earlier or later.
  Pick one sound track or mix them all. The video is copied as it is, so it's quick and loses no
  quality.
- **Clean up audio:** one click to "make it sound better", or pick: background noise reduction
  (with a strength), 50 or 60 Hz hum removal, even out the volume for streaming, podcasts, TV or
  your own level, and voice clarity, bass boost or rumble removal. Listen before and after, and
  see the loudness before and after. Works on audio files too.
- **Remove silence:** finds the quiet parts (you choose how quiet and how long) and shows them on
  the timeline; turn each one on or off. Then cut them out, or speed them up instead, and see the
  time saved. Works on audio files too.

### Look & overlays

- **Filters:** one-click looks (warm, cool, faded, noir, vivid, retro, black & white, sepia,
  dreamy, punchy), fades in and out from black or white, and under advanced brightness,
  contrast, saturation, exposure, temperature, tint, hue, highlights, shadows, sharpen, vignette
  and blur, for the whole video or one part. A live preview while you choose, and a before/after
  slider at the end.
- **Add text:** titles, lower thirds (name and role), captions and end cards, as many as you
  like. Pick a font (or use your own font file), size, color, outline, shadow, a box behind it,
  alignment, position, rotation, opacity, when it shows, and a fade or slide in.
- **Watermark:** your logo (PNG, JPEG, WebP or SVG) or text, in a corner or the center, with a
  margin, size and opacity, or repeated across the whole video. Show it for the whole video or
  one part. Save it as your watermark to use again.
- **Blur region:** hide faces, number plates or private details with blur, pixels or a black
  box, as rectangles or ellipses with soft edges, for a chosen time. For something that moves,
  pin its position at a few times and the region slides between them. You can also mute or
  bleep the sound while it's hidden.

### Subtitles

- **Subtitle editor:** open SRT, WebVTT, ASS/SSA, SBV, TTML or a plain transcript (files in older
  encodings are read correctly too), with or without the video. Edit each line's text and times,
  or drag them on the timeline; add a line at the playhead, split and merge lines. **Tap to
  time:** press T at the start and end of each line while the video plays. Lines that are too
  fast to read or overlap are flagged. Undo and redo, and your work is saved in the browser as
  you go. Save as any of the formats. **Resync:** shift everything, or fix two lines' times and
  stretch the rest, or fix a 23.976 ↔ 25 fps mismatch. Clean up [music] and (laughs) notes and
  wrap long lines.
- **Add subtitles:** add subtitle tracks viewers can turn on and off (MP4, MKV or WebM, several
  languages, a default track) without re-encoding; burn them into the picture with your font,
  size, color, outline, box and position; or **social style**, one to three words at a time with
  the current word highlighted. Or pull out the subtitles already inside a video as SRT.
- **Auto captions:** speech to text with Whisper, running in your browser. Pick the tiny, base or
  small model (downloaded once, size shown first), the language or automatic, and optionally
  translate to English. Get a transcript where clicking a line jumps to it, download SRT, VTT or
  TXT, or send the captions straight to the subtitle editor or add subtitles.

### Info and repair

- **Media info:** drop one or more files to see their format, size, length, bitrate, codecs,
  resolution, frame rate, rotation, sound details and metadata. It warns in plain words about
  things that often cause trouble: variable frame rate, not optimized for streaming, a codec this
  browser can't play, HDR, and rotation flags. Copy everything as text or download it as JSON. A
  panel shows what this browser can play and make.
- **Metadata:** see and edit a file's title, artist, comment, description, dates and every other
  stored detail. Location and camera or phone details are pointed out, each with a remove button,
  or remove all metadata at once for privacy (no re-encoding). Replace or remove an audio file's
  cover art.

### Create

- **Slideshow:** drop photos, sort them by name or date, reorder them, and set how long each
  shows and a caption. Fit them with a blurred background, black bars or a crop, with a gentle
  zoom. Add transitions and music (it fades in and out), or fit the slides to the music. 16:9,
  9:16 or 1:1.
- **Screen recorder:** record your screen, a window or a tab (with its sound where the browser
  allows), your webcam, your screen with a round webcam bubble, or just your voice. A 3-2-1
  countdown, pause and resume, a timer and a sound level meter. The recording is saved as it
  goes, so a crash doesn't lose it. Afterwards, watch it, trim it, download it, or open it in
  another tool.
- **Batch:** do the same thing to many files or a whole folder: convert, compress, resize,
  rotate, extract audio, add your saved watermark, clean up audio, remove all metadata, make a
  GIF, or make for a platform. A queue shows each file's progress and new size; pause, cancel
  and retry. Get one ZIP or save straight into a folder, named by your own pattern.

### The video engine

The browser does most of the work itself, using its own (usually hardware) video encoder. For
formats it can't read or make, tools offer to load the **video engine**, a copy of ffmpeg that
also runs on your device. It's about 32 MB, downloaded once when you first need it and then kept
on your device. Tools say which engine they'll use and why before you start.

While anything is being made, the screen stays awake, the tab's title shows the progress, and
closing the tab asks first. Results bigger than about 1 GB can be saved straight to disk (in
Chrome and Edge) instead of being held in memory. Several results download as one ZIP.

## Installation

Requires Node 22.22+ (Node 24 recommended; see `.nvmrc`).

```sh
npm install
```

## Usage

```sh
npm run dev        # http://localhost:5173
```

Open a tool from the home page, drop in a file, and press the button at the bottom of the
settings. In merge, drop in two or more videos, set the part of each to use, arrange them in the
preview (space plays and pauses), and press **export mp4**.

### Browsers

Everything works best in recent **Chrome or Edge** on a desktop: they have hardware video
encoders, WebGPU (faster auto captions), recording straight to MP4, and saving straight to disk
or into a folder. **Safari** works for nearly everything. **Firefox** works, but auto captions are
slower (no WebGPU) and screen recordings are saved as WebM. MP4, MOV, WebM and MKV are read by
the browser; other formats go through the video engine.

### Hosting

The video engine runs fastest with several threads, which browsers only allow on pages served
with these headers (the dev and preview servers already send them):

```
Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy: same-origin
Cross-Origin-Embedder-Policy: require-corp
```

Without them it still works, on a single thread. Ready-made configs send them: `netlify.toml`
(Netlify), `vercel.json` (Vercel) and `public/_headers` (Cloudflare Pages, also read by
Netlify). **GitHub Pages** can't set headers, so the engine runs on one thread there (slower,
but it works). The engine's files are copied into
`dist/ffmpeg/<version>/` by the build and served by the app itself; no CDN is used. The
`.wasm` files are about 32 MB each, so hosts with a per-file limit below that won't work.
